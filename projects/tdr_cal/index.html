Introduction

Background: TDR Setup in PCB Manufacturing

Time Domain Reflectometry (TDR) is widely used in substrate and PCB manufacturing to control the impedance of transmission lines. Figure 1 illustrates a typical test setup for measuring differential transmission lines on a substrate or PCB, where the device under test (DUT) is accessed using PCB probes. These probes convert the coaxial test interface into a coplanar structure suitable for the DUT. Due to environmental constraints that prevent positioning the TDR equipment close to the DUT, long cables are often used to extend the connection. Before testing, the TDR is typically calibrated using a 50 Ω standard to eliminate reflections. However, this calibration does not account for signal loss introduced by the cables.


Issue Overview

Measurement errors introduced by the cable become significant when the DUT impedance deviates substantially from the system impedance—typically 50 Ω for single-ended or 100 Ω for differential signals. This occurs because the reflected energy from the DUT is attenuated as it travels back through the cable. As a result, the reflection magnitude appears smaller, causing the measured impedance to be incorrectly interpreted as closer to the system impedance. Figure 2 illustrates this effect: a 50 Ω differential transmission line, verified using a fully calibrated VNA with SOLT calibration, shows a 20% error in TDR measurement due to cable-induced reflection loss.


Correction Methods

To accurately account for the effects of the cable and test fixture, an open-short-load (SOL) calibration using a VNA or TDR can be performed. However, due to the high cost of these instruments, this approach may not be feasible.

Another approach is to use machine learning techniques to predict the actual impedance based on cable characterization. Details of this method can be found in the paper.
